# a*life

A minimal life-sim sandbox for iOS and macOS, built with SwiftUI and RealityKit. A little character walks around a 64×64 lot. Tap a tile and the character finds its way there with A* pathfinding, around the walls of the office in the middle of the lot, and around whatever has been built and planted there.

The lot also shows your agents: one character per session. Each character is a Claude Code session of its own, run by the Claude Code on your Mac: you talk to it directly, it asks you before it does anything risky, and what you tell one never reaches the others. The office is a modern glass tower with neon along its edges, with a room for each character, lit in the colour of its shirt, and a storey for every two of them. A character walks into the office while it works, takes the lift at the back of the lobby, and after a moment appears at the desk in its own room, by the window, where you can see it through the glass. It stays there with a "!" over its head while it waits for your answer, and once it's done, and you've heard what it had to say, it comes back down and waits on the lawn. If it finished while you were too far away to hear it, a phone rings over its head until you take its call. When a character sends a helper off to do part of its work (a subagent, in Claude Code terms), a little sheep in the character's colour pops up beside it in its room and stays there with it. Once the helper has reported back, the sheep swells up like a balloon and pops, with a bang, into a puff of scraps. What the Builder builds and the Gardener plants appears on the lot: tell the Builder to build a restaurant and a restaurant pops up, with a striped awning and its name over it, or a barn, with chickens and goats in a pen and horses that gallop round the lot and come back to feed; ask the Gardener for roses or an oak and they grow there, or for foals, which trot, frisk, graze and lie down round their patch. The sessions live on a small server you run yourself, [`a-life-server`](a-life-server), which the apps reach over Tailscale.

The lot sits in a box, like a diorama. Four walls stand on its edges with a world painted on their insides: behind the office, where the camera starts out looking, a beach and a bay, with a city of towers on the horizon across the water; on the other side, snowy mountains behind a forest of giant redwoods; and in between, the coast turning into wooded hills. Over the top is a flat sky, painted on the walls above the world and over them, with a day of its own: a day on the lot lasts five hours, and the time of day follows the clock, so it is the same in every app and when you come back. The sun rises in the east, crosses the sky and sets in the west, warm and low at dawn and dusk; at night the moon comes up opposite it, the stars come out, the world on the walls dims to a moonlit blue, and the city's windows light up.

## Run it

Open `ALife.xcodeproj` in Xcode 16 or later, pick the **ALife** scheme, and run it on an iPhone/iPad simulator or **My Mac**. To run on a device, set your team under *Signing & Capabilities*.

Requires iOS 18 / macOS 15, the first releases where `RealityView` and entity-targeted gestures work outside visionOS.

| | iOS / iPadOS | macOS |
|---|---|---|
| Walk | Tap a tile | Click a tile |
| Orbit camera | Drag | Drag |
| Zoom | Pinch | Scroll wheel or trackpad pinch |

The camera glides after you to wherever you walk, and over to anything that has just been built or planted. It never leaves the box: zoom out and it stops at the walls, and under the sky, and looks down at the lot from there, so it reaches the opposite edge of the lot at the most, and you never see behind a wall. Tilt it down until it is nearly level to look across the lot at the walls, and keep tilting to look up at the sky, nearly straight up.

## The agents

The front ends don't run agents themselves. They follow each character's session on the server and send it what you say.

1. Start the server on your Mac: `cd a-life-server && swift run ALifeServer`. It needs [Claude Code](https://code.claude.com) installed and logged in; the characters use your login and count towards your usage. (`ALIFE_BACKEND=dummy` runs scripted stand-ins instead, which cost nothing.)
2. Run the app, open the settings (the gear at the top) and set the server's address. On the same Mac or in the simulator, press **Use this machine**. From another device, enter the HTTPS address `tailscale serve` gives you; [`a-life-server/README.md`](a-life-server/README.md) walks through that.
3. Tap a character, or its name in the panel at the bottom, and tell it what to do. Tell the **Builder** "build a restaurant" and it sends its construction worker off, a helper that builds the restaurant as a file in the lot's `buildings/` directory; the construction worker asks before it writes (Allow or Deny), and the Builder can't be told anything else until you answer. Once it is written, the restaurant appears on the lot. Ask the **Gardener** to plant roses or an oak, or to put out a few foals, and it sends its planter off, which plants them in `garden/`, and they grow on the lot; changes to the lot go ahead without asking. The **Engineer**, in the purple shirt, works on a\*life itself rather than on the lot: its session works in your a\*life checkout, and when you ask it to fix or add something in the app or the server it sends its a\*life expert off, a helper that knows a\*life inside out, to make the change and run the tests. The a\*life expert asks before every change and command, and leaves its changes in your checkout for you to look over; [the server's README](a-life-server/README.md#the-engineer-and-the-alife-checkout) has more. You can talk to a character while it works, too: Claude Code takes it up at its next step and carries on.

The characters share the lot, a directory on your Mac (`~/a-life-lot` unless you set `ALIFE_LOT_DIR`), so the Gardener can see where the Builder has built. Each building and bed starts with a few lines that say what it is and where it stands; the server reads them, keeps the two from landing on each other or on the house, and sends the lot to the apps. [The server's README](a-life-server/README.md#the-lot) has the details. Nobody hands out the work for you: you are the one telling each character what to do and carrying news between them. Who is on the lot, and what each one is told, is set in the server's agents file, [`agents.yaml`](a-life-server/Sources/ALifeServer/agents.yaml): change a character, add one or take one away there, and restart the server ([its README](a-life-server/README.md#the-agents-file) describes the format). Or ask the **Team Lead**, in teal, for someone new: it works out with you who they are, what they do and where, and sends its recruiter off to add them to the agents file, which asks you first. They join the lot when you restart the server. The Team Lead only adds agents: it leaves the ones there as they are, and doesn't build, plant or change code ([The Team Lead](a-life-server/README.md#the-team-lead-and-the-agents-file) has more). A character there can also work on a project of your own rather than the lot, a directory on your Mac or one or more git repositories the server clones, so that you can hand it the work on another app; [A character for another project](a-life-server/README.md#a-character-for-another-project) shows how. The **Flamingo Engineer**, in pink, is one: it looks after Flamingo Chess, whose iOS app, Light Phone app and server the server clones side by side for it. Your own character, the one labelled "You", is the one that walks where you tap.

Every front end connected to the same server sees the same sessions. If the connection drops, the app reconnects and the server sends what it missed.

### Hearing them

The characters say what they have to say out loud, each in a voice of its own, but only while your character stands within 3 tiles of theirs: walk over to the office to hear the ones at their desks. Out of earshot, they keep quiet, but what they say is kept for you: walk away mid-sentence and the character stops, and what you didn't hear is kept too. Once a character has finished its job and you missed some of it, it calls you instead: a green phone rings over its head, in its room, and the panel shows an incoming call from it with a **Take** button (and how many more are holding, if others are calling too). Take the call to hear what you missed in its voice, wherever you are; walking over doesn't answer it. A character with a call, or with lines still to be said, stays at its desk until you have heard it out, or until you tell it something new, which hangs up its call unheard. Calls are kept by the app, so they don't outlast it. Only what a character says to you is spoken, read as plain prose: not your own messages, its tool calls, its errors, or its code. Lines are said one at a time, in the order they came.

The voices are [Phonon](https://github.com/gradium-ai/xn-ptts) text to speech, which runs on the device, on Apple silicon. The app downloads the voice model (about 160 MB) from the server the first time, and keeps it; for that, fetch it onto the server's Mac once with `a-life-server/fetch-voice.sh` and restart the server ([Voices](a-life-server/README.md#voices) has the details). Settings shows how the voices are getting on, the last line said out loud, and why a line wasn't, if one wasn't. On an iPhone or iPad the voices play with the silent switch on, at the media volume, alongside whatever else is playing.

## How it's put together

| File | What it does |
|---|---|
| `ALifeApp.swift` | App entry point. Registers the custom components and systems, and owns the `ServerConnection`. |
| `WorldView.swift` | The `RealityView`, plus the gestures (tap a character to talk to it, a tile to walk there) and the macOS scroll-wheel monitor. Hands the world the sessions and the lot, and the narrator what the characters say, who is still working and who is in earshot; keeps a character at its desk while the narrator still has something from it for you. |
| `World.swift` | Builds the 64×64 lot from `LotPlan` (the floor, the office's walls, bushes, your character, the sunlight) with the land, the sea and the sky round it, turns a tapped tile into a walk, shows what's built and planted, and keeps one character per session, with a ring under the one you're talking to, a room in the office, a green phone ringing over its head while it calls you, and a sheep in its room for each helper it sends off. |
| `Office.swift` | The office the characters work in, on the house's tiles in the middle of the lot and laid out by `OfficePlan`: a glass tower with steel columns and neon along its edges and roof, a lobby with the doorway at the front, a reception desk and a lift at the back, and a storey for every two characters over it. Each character's room is in its shirt's colour: the carpet, the back wall, the neon and a light, with a desk by the window. It is built again when the characters on the lot change. |
| `Buildings.swift` | The Builder's buildings, drawn by kind: a house with a pitched roof and a chimney, a restaurant, cafe or shop with a striped awning, an office or a block of flats with bands of windows, a tower as a wind turbine with turning blades and a blinking red light on top, a school or shed, a barn with its farmyard and horses (see `Barn.swift`), a church with a spire, a hospital with a red cross, and a plain building for anything else, each with its name over it. |
| `Plants.swift` | The Gardener's beds, drawn by kind, one plant on each tile: flowers in their colour (roses, tulips, daisies, tall sunflowers, lavender), trees (oak, birch, apple, cherry, maple, pine, palm), bushes, hedges, grass and vegetables (carrots, lettuces, cabbages, tomatoes, pumpkins), and a little green plant for anything else. |
| `AgentPanel.swift` | The status pill, a button per character (or, where they don't all fit, one button with a menu of everyone, each in their shirt's colour, and an orange badge counting the others waiting for you), an incoming call bar with a Take button for the characters calling you, and the conversation with the one you picked, which folds down to a bar of the characters' statuses: the whole conversation in a view that scrolls (what was said, the tools the character used, and its requests for a yes or no, with Allow and Deny while they wait and what was chosen afterwards), the message field (sending hangs up that character's call), Stop and Start over. |
| `SettingsView.swift` | Sets the server's address, and shows how the voices are getting on and the last line they said. |
| `Voices.swift` | The characters' voices: downloads the Phonon voice model from the server once, loads it, and speaks a line in the voice that goes with each character, through the `SpeechEngine` protocol the `Narrator` uses. |
| `CameraRig.swift` | Orbit camera: a pivot entity that glides over the lot to where it's told to look, and a `PerspectiveCamera` that turns and tilts round it on a boom, or looks up at the sky once tilted past level. `CameraRigComponent` holds the turn, tilt and zoom, and `CameraRigSystem` places the camera every frame, where `CameraReach` says it may be for the pivot's position just then, easing it there over a few frames, so it stays inside the box while the pivot glides. |
| `Horizon.swift` | The world painted on the insides of the walls, laid out by `HorizonPlan`: on the south wall a bay with a beach and the surf along it, on the north wall mountains with rocky faces and snowy tops over foothills and a forest of giant redwoods (tall red trunks, narrow dark crowns), and on the east and west walls the coast turning into hills covered in pines, mixed strip by strip where they meet. It is painted in flat layers a few centimetres apart, in front of the sky, each shape lit on one side and in shade on the other, in one mesh with a part per colour, built with `WallPainter` and `MeshBatch`. Its colours are dimmed and warmed with the time of day (`PaintComponent`). Nothing in it takes taps, and it stands behind the outermost tiles, so the characters walk in front of it. |
| `Skyline.swift` | The city on the horizon across the bay, painted on the south wall: two rows of towers on a quay, taller in the middle and at the west end, each with a sliver of its side in shade, some with water tanks, and a few that stand out: a stepped stone tower with a mast, one with a crown of shining tiers and a needle, a tapering glass supertall with a spire, pencil-thin towers and a glass slab. Bands of windows on every floor, about half of which light up at night, and red lights blinking on the tallest. |
| `SkyBox.swift` | The sky: painted on the walls behind the world on them, from the grass to the top, and on a flat sky over the box, coloured by a gradient drawn again through the day, by how high each spot looks from the middle of the lot. The sun's and the moon's discs and about 1,600 stars are painted where you would see them from there (`HorizonPlan.skyPoint`), and the soft light from all round (an image-based light) that the lot takes in. `SkyComponent` holds its parts. |
| `DaySystem.swift` | ECS: `NightLightsComponent`, `PaintComponent` and a `System` that, twice a second, brings the day up to date from `DayClock`: moves the sunlight from east to west, warm at dawn and dusk, and turns it into a dim blue moonlight at night, never lower than a little over the horizon so shadows stay sensible; paints the sun and the moon on the sky, fades the stars in and paints them again as they turn round the pole, colours the sky, dims the soft light, lights the world on the walls for the time of day, and lights the city's windows. |
| `WalkSystem.swift` | ECS: `TileComponent`, `LotComponent` (the floor plan, on the lot's root, with routes between tiles), `WalkComponent`, and a `System` that moves walkers along their waypoints every frame. |
| `CommuteSystem.swift` | ECS: `CommuteComponent` and a `System` that takes each character to work and back: in through the door to the lift, a moment and a half out of sight on the way up, then at the desk in its room while it works, and back down the lift and out to its spot on the lawn once it's done. `Commute` decides when. |
| `SheepSystem.swift` | ECS: `SheepComponent` and a `System` that takes each helper's sheep from popping up beside its character's desk, through staying in the room with it (looking at it, and hopping now and then), to swelling up like a balloon and bursting once the helper has reported back. |
| `Burst.swift` | ECS: `BurstComponent` and a `System` that flies a burst sheep's scraps of wool and scarf out, down and away, and the pop that goes with it, a crack and a thump made in code rather than a sound file. |
| `Foals.swift` | The Gardener's foals: a bed whose kind is a foal (or a pony, a colt, a filly or a baby horse) has one on each tile, on a patch of long grass that anyone can walk across. Each is built like a barn's horse at half the size, with long legs, a short body, a big head and a stubby tail, in the bed's colour or a coat of its own, as the bed's child. |
| `Barn.swift` | A barn (or a stable or a farm) and its farmyard, on the barn's own tiles: a red barn with big braced doors and a hayloft, a trough of hay in front, a fenced pen of chickens and goats beside it and, if there's room, a round lunging ring with a sand floor. Two or three horses come with it, as the barn's children, so they go when it does. |
| `BarnSystem.swift` | ECS: `HorseComponent`, `FoalComponent`, `PenAnimalComponent` and a `System` that takes each horse from feeding at the trough, head down, to galloping off to a few places round the lot (on routes round whatever stands there), back to the trough, and now and then, one at a time, through the gate and round the lunging ring; their legs swing and their bodies bob as they go. The foals stay within a few tiles of their bed: they graze, trot or dash off, buck and kick up their heels, and lie down for a rest. The chickens potter about the pen, pecking, and the goats graze. |
| `Phone.swift` | The green handset that rings over a character calling you, and ECS: `RingComponent` and a `System` that rattles it in bursts, like a phone's bell. |
| `TurbineSystem.swift` | ECS: `SpinComponent`, `BlinkComponent` and a `System` that turns a wind turbine's rotor and blinks the red light on top of it every frame. |
| `TileGrid.swift` | Plain Swift with no RealityKit: the floor plan and A* (8-directional, no corner cutting). |

Every floor tile is its own entity with a `CollisionComponent` and an `InputTargetComponent`. A tap therefore hits the tile directly, so the code never has to turn a screen point into a world position. The grass itself is drawn as two meshes, one for the light tiles and one for the dark, so the 4,096 tiles don't cost 4,096 draw calls.

### The `ALifeKit` package

`ALifeKit/` is a local Swift package with no dependencies and two modules. The app also uses Phonon's `ptts` package for its voices; it is Apple silicon only, so the app isn't built for Intel.

| Module | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `ALifeAPI` | The wire format: `Message`, `Approval`, `Subagent`, `SessionEvent`, the request bodies, and `SessionState`, which turns a session's events into what a front end shows of that character; and the lot: `LotPlan` (its size, and the house and lawn in the middle), `LotArea`, and `Lot` with its `LotItem`s, everything built and planted, with `isFoals` for a bed of the Gardener's foals. The server uses this module too, so the two can't drift apart. |
| `ALifeClient` | What a front end uses. `ServerConnection` follows every session on a server: `connect()`, `setServerURL(_:)`, `startEverySessionOver()`, `phase`, `sessions`, one `AgentSession` per character, and `lot`, what's built and planted. An `AgentSession` is the observable handle on one character: `send(_:)`, `interrupt()`, `respond(to:allow:)`, `startOver()`, and the state to draw (`status`, `activity`, `transcript`, `conversation`, `pendingApprovals`, `isWaitingForYou`, `subagents`, `lastError`). `ALifeServerClient` makes the HTTP calls and reads the event streams. `Narrator` says the characters' new lines (an `AgentSession`'s `newMessages`) out loud through a `SpeechEngine`, while they are within `Earshot.distance` of you, keeps the lines you missed and turns them into a `Call` once the character's job is done (`calls`, `take(callFrom:)`, `hangUp(on:)`, and `hasSomethingToSay(_:)` to keep it at its desk till then), and `SpokenText` turns a message's Markdown into what is said. `OfficePlan` lays out the office: how many storeys it has for so many characters, which room is whose, by slot, where the lift is, and where a helper's sheep stands in its character's room. `Commute` decides when a character goes up to its room and when it comes back out: only once it has looked done for a second, so it stays at its desk through the moment between you answering its request and it running again. `DayClock` turns the date into the time of day on the lot, a five-hour day the same for everyone, and its `Sky` says where the sun and the moon are (`elevation`, `azimuth`) and how light it is (`daylight`, `glow` at dawn and dusk, `moonlight`, `starlight`). `HorizonPlan` lays out the lot's box (the compass, the walls on the lot's edges and how tall they are, what is painted on them, and where the sky shows a direction), and `CameraReach` how the camera moves inside it: how far it tilts, looks up and zooms, and where the walls and the sky stop it; tests check that from every camera it allows, it is inside the box and everything it sees is the lot, a wall or the sky, that it keeps the point it turns round in view, and that it moves without jumps. |

### The server

[`a-life-server/`](a-life-server) is a separate Swift package (Vapor). Its README covers running it, Tailscale, the API, and how each character's Claude Code session and its helper are set up.

### Tests

```sh
swift test --package-path ALifeKit        # the client against a stubbed server
swift test --package-path a-life-server   # the client against the real server
```

## Ideas for next steps

- Swap `TileGrid` for RealityKit's navigation mesh (`NavigationMeshResource` / `NavigationComponent` / `NavigationController`, new at WWDC26) once you can raise the deployment target. `TileGrid.path` already returns the same thing `computePath` does: `nil` if there's no path, `[]` if you're already there.
- Replace the box character with a rigged USDZ that has walk and idle animations.
- Build mode: snap furniture to the grid and call `grid.block(_:)` for each piece.
- Keyboard camera controls on macOS with `.onKeyPress`.
