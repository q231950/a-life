# a*life

A minimal life-sim sandbox for iOS and macOS, built with SwiftUI and RealityKit. A little character walks around a 64×64 lot. Tap a tile and the character finds its way there with A* pathfinding, around the walls of the office in the middle of the lot, and around whatever has been built and planted there.

The lot also shows your agents: one character per session. Each character is a Claude Code session of its own, run by the Claude Code on your Mac or on a server of your own: you talk to it directly, it asks you before it does anything risky, and what you tell one never reaches the others. The office is a modern glass tower with neon along its edges, with a room for each character, lit in the colour of its shirt, and a storey for every two of them. A character walks into the office while it works, takes the lift at the back of the lobby, and after a moment appears at the desk in its own room, by the window, where you can see it through the glass. It stays there with a "!" over its head while it waits for your answer, and once it's done, and you've heard what it had to say, it comes back down and waits on the lawn. If it finished while you were too far away to hear it, a phone rings over its head until you take its call, and if it finished while you were away, it leaves you a missed call to call back. When a character sends a helper off to do part of its work (a subagent, in Claude Code terms), a little sheep in the character's colour pops up beside it in its room and stays there with it. Once the helper has reported back, the sheep swells up like a balloon and pops, with a bang, into a puff of scraps. What the Builder builds and the Conservationist plants or puts out appears on the lot: tell the Builder to build a restaurant and a restaurant pops up, with a striped awning and its name over it, or a barn, with chickens and goats in a pen and horses that gallop round the lot and come back to feed; ask the Conservationist for roses or an oak and they grow there, or for animals: foals, which trot, frisk, graze and lie down round their patch, brown bears and bigger polar bears, which amble about and rear up, unicorns with a golden horn, and birds and winged pegasuses, which fly up and round over their bed, hover and land again, or for dinosaurs: stegosauruses with plates down their backs, long-necked diplodocuses, triceratops with a frill and three horns, tyrannosauruses that stalk about on two legs and roar, and crested pterosaurs, which fly like the birds. The sessions live on a small server you run yourself, [`a-life-server`](a-life-server), on your Mac or on a Linux server of your own, which the apps reach over Tailscale.

The lot sits in a box, like a diorama. Four walls stand on its edges with a world painted on their insides: behind the office, where the camera starts out looking, a beach and a bay, with a city of towers on the horizon across the water; on the other side, snowy mountains behind a forest of giant redwoods; and in between, the coast turning into wooded hills. Over the top is a flat sky, painted on the walls above the world and over them, with a day of its own: a day on the lot is Claude's 5-hour usage window, from midnight as the window starts, through noon halfway, to midnight again as it resets, so it is the same in every app and when you come back. Between windows the lot stays at midnight until a character's run starts a new one, and the sky sweeps over to it in about ten seconds rather than jumping. Until the server has read any window at all, the day runs on five-hour days that follow the clock alone. The sun rises in the east, crosses the sky and sets in the west, warm and low at dawn and dusk, while a few soft clouds drift slowly round the sky, white by day and warm at dawn and dusk; at night the moon comes up opposite it with a soft glow round it, the stars come out on the sky and the walls, the clouds turn dark and faint, the world on the walls dims to a moonlit blue, and the city's windows light up. The box's edges show gently, by day and by night: the walls, the sky and the lot are shaded softly towards the corners where they meet, with a faint bevel along each.

## Run it

Open `ALife.xcodeproj` in Xcode 16 or later, pick the **ALife** scheme, and run it on an iPhone/iPad simulator or **My Mac**. To run on a device, set your team under *Signing & Capabilities*.

Requires iOS 18 / macOS 15, the first releases where `RealityView` and entity-targeted gestures work outside visionOS.

| | iOS / iPadOS | macOS |
|---|---|---|
| Walk | Tap a tile | Click a tile |
| Orbit camera | Drag | Drag |
| Zoom | Pinch | Scroll wheel or trackpad pinch |

The camera glides after you to wherever you walk, and over to anything that has just been built or planted. It never leaves the box: zoom out and it stops at the walls, and under the sky, and looks down at the lot from there, so it reaches the opposite edge of the lot at the most, and you never see behind a wall. Tilt it down until it is nearly level to look across the lot at the walls, and keep tilting to look up at the sky, nearly straight up.

## The agents

The front ends don't run agents themselves. They follow each character's session on the server and send it what you say.

1. Start the server on your Mac: `cd a-life-server && swift run ALifeServer`. It needs [Claude Code](https://code.claude.com) installed and logged in; the characters use your login and count towards your usage. (`ALIFE_BACKEND=dummy` runs scripted stand-ins instead, which cost nothing.) Or run it on a server of your own, such as a DigitalOcean droplet, with Docker and your Claude subscription's token, so the characters carry on while your Mac sleeps: [Run it on a server](a-life-server/README.md#run-it-on-a-server) walks you through it. Each person runs their own server; it has no login, so keep it to yourself, on your tailnet.
2. Run the app, open the settings (the gear at the top) and set the server's address. On the same Mac or in the simulator, press **Use this machine**. From another device, enter the HTTPS address `tailscale serve` gives you; [`a-life-server/README.md`](a-life-server/README.md) walks through that.
3. Tap a character, or its name in the panel at the bottom, and tell it what to do. Tell the **Builder** "build a restaurant" and it sends its construction worker off, a helper that builds the restaurant as a file in the lot's `buildings/` directory; the construction worker asks before it writes (Allow or Deny), and the Builder can't be told anything else until you answer. Once it is written, the restaurant appears on the lot. Ask the **Conservationist**, in green, to plant roses or an oak, or to put out a few foals, bears, polar bears, birds, unicorns, pegasuses or dinosaurs (stegosauruses, diplodocuses, pterosaurs, triceratops or tyrannosauruses), and it sends its keeper off, which plants them in `garden/`, and they grow on the lot; changes to the lot go ahead without asking. The **Engineer**, in the purple shirt, works on a\*life itself rather than on the lot: its session works in your a\*life checkout, and when you ask it to fix or add something in the app or the server it sends its a\*life expert off, a helper that knows a\*life inside out, to make the change and run the tests. The a\*life expert asks before every change and command, and leaves its changes in your checkout for you to look over; [the server's README](a-life-server/README.md#the-engineer-and-the-alife-checkout) has more. You can talk to a character while it works, too: Claude Code takes it up at its next step and carries on.

A small panel at the top left shows how much of the 5-hour usage window is used, as a ring that fills clockwise round its edge (green, amber from 70%, red from 90%) with the percentage in the middle, and how long until it resets. The server reads it from what Claude Code says on every turn of a character's run, so it can't see what your other Claude sessions use: a reading over half an hour old is dimmed and says when it was taken ("as of 14:02"). The panel isn't there until the server has a reading, nor once that window has reset, until a run reports a new one.

If a character's job runs into Claude's 5-hour usage limit, it waits for the limit to reset and then carries on by itself, even with the app closed: a minute after the reset, the server tells it "please continue" in the same session, which shows in the conversation as a message from a\*life. Meanwhile its status reads "Back at 15:01", and the log says when it hit the limit and when it was told to carry on. Telling it anything yourself, stopping it or starting it over calls that off. The server keeps who is waiting in a small file, so a restart doesn't forget them; the weekly limit is only reported. [The usage limit](a-life-server/README.md#the-usage-limit) has the details.

The characters share the lot, a directory on your Mac (`~/a-life-lot` unless you set `ALIFE_LOT_DIR`), so the Conservationist can see where the Builder has built. Each building and bed starts with a few lines that say what it is and where it stands; the server reads them, keeps the two from landing on each other or on the house, and sends the lot to the apps. [The server's README](a-life-server/README.md#the-lot) has the details. Nobody hands out the work for you: you are the one telling each character what to do and carrying news between them. Who is on the lot, what each one is told and the model it runs on (Haiku for the Builder and the Conservationist, Sonnet for the Team Lead, Opus for the engineers; the Model choice in the conversation's More menu changes it) is set in the server's agents file, [`agents.yaml`](a-life-server/Sources/ALifeServer/agents.yaml): change a character, add one or take one away there, and restart the server ([its README](a-life-server/README.md#the-agents-file) describes the format). Or ask the **Team Lead**, in teal, for someone new: it works out with you who they are, what they do and where, and sends its recruiter off to add them to the agents file, which asks you first. They join the lot when you restart the server. The Team Lead only adds agents: it leaves the ones there as they are, and doesn't build, plant or change code ([The Team Lead](a-life-server/README.md#the-team-lead-and-the-agents-file) has more). A character there can also work on a project of your own rather than the lot, a directory on your Mac or one or more git repositories the server clones, so that you can hand it the work on another app; [A character for another project](a-life-server/README.md#a-character-for-another-project) shows how. The **Flamingo Engineer**, in pink, is one: it looks after Flamingo Chess, whose iOS app, Light Phone app and server the server clones side by side for it. Your own character, the one labelled "You", is the one that walks where you tap.

Every front end connected to the same server sees the same sessions. If the connection drops, the app reconnects and the server sends what it missed.

### Hearing them

The characters say what they have to say out loud, each in a voice of its own, but only while your character stands within 3 tiles of theirs: walk over to the office to hear the ones at their desks. Out of earshot, they keep quiet, but what they say is kept for you: walk away mid-sentence and the character stops, and what you didn't hear is kept too. Once a character has finished its job and you missed some of it, it calls you instead: a green phone rings over its head, in its room, and the panel shows an incoming call from it with a **Take** button (and how many more are holding, if others are calling too). Take the call to hear what you missed in its voice, wherever you are; walking over doesn't answer it. A character with a call, or with lines still to be said, stays at its desk until you have heard it out, or until you tell it something new, which hangs up its call unheard. Calls are kept by the app, so they don't outlast it, but each one is written down in the log too, so hanging up loses nothing. Only what a character says to you is spoken, read as plain prose: not your own messages, its tool calls, its errors, or its code. Lines are said one at a time, in the order they came.

What happened while the app was closed is never said out loud or rung about. That goes for what the app catches up on when it connects, and for a job a character already had when you came, until it is done (or until you answer it or tell it something): its helpers reporting back and what it says meanwhile are only written down. The list button in the status pill at the top opens the log of what the characters have been up to, newest first: their replies, the helpers they sent off and that came back, the jobs they finished or were stopped in, the usage limits they waited out and being told to carry on, what they asked you, their errors and their calls, missed ones too. A red badge counts what happened while you were away and you haven't seen (what the characters do while you watch is logged but not badged), and what happened while you were away is picked out at the top under **While you were away**. Tap an entry to talk to that character. Opening the log marks everything in it as seen, and the app remembers that between launches.

A character that finished a job while you were away (it ended with nothing more to do, or it is waiting for your answer) leaves you a missed call instead, without a sound: one per character, however much it said. The panel shows a missed call from it with a **Call back** button (and how many more were missed), and its button has a red phone beside its name. Call it back to hear its replies from that job in its voice, wherever you are, just like taking a call. Opening its conversation clears the missed call, since you can read what it said there, and so does telling it something new. The app remembers the last reply you heard from each character between launches, so a job you heard out, or a missed call you cleared, isn't a missed call again next time; a call you left ringing when you closed the app is. Each missed call is written down in the log, under While you were away.

The voices are [Phonon](https://github.com/gradium-ai/xn-ptts) text to speech, which runs on the device, on Apple silicon. The app downloads the voice model (about 160 MB) from the server the first time, and keeps it; for that, fetch it onto the server's Mac once with `a-life-server/fetch-voice.sh` and restart the server ([Voices](a-life-server/README.md#voices) has the details). Settings shows how the voices are getting on, the last line said out loud, and why a line wasn't, if one wasn't. On an iPhone or iPad the voices play with the silent switch on, at the media volume, alongside whatever else is playing.

Every floor tile is its own entity with a `CollisionComponent` and an `InputTargetComponent`. A tap therefore hits the tile directly, so the code never has to turn a screen point into a world position. The grass itself is drawn as two meshes, one for the light tiles and one for the dark, so the 4,096 tiles don't cost 4,096 draw calls.

### The `ALifeKit` package

`ALifeKit/` is a local Swift package with no dependencies and two modules. The app also uses Phonon's `ptts` package for its voices; it is Apple silicon only, so the app isn't built for Intel.

| Module | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `ALifeAPI` | The wire format: `Message`, `Approval`, `Subagent`, `SessionEvent`, the request bodies, and `SessionState`, which turns a session's events into what a front end shows of that character; `SessionContext`, how full a character's session is and the model playing it (`model`, `usedTokens`, `compactsAt`, `contextWindow`, `autoCompact`); `ClaudeModel`, the models a character can run on by their simple names (`fable`, `opus`, `sonnet`, `haiku`), which a `SessionState` has as `model` and `fileModel`, the agents file's; `ModelOption`, one of them as the server's Claude Code describes it (`name` with its version, `description`, `resolved`); `ModelChoiceRequest`; `Usage`, with its last `UsageWindow` (`utilization`, `resetsAt`, `readAt` and `startedAt`); and the lot: `LotPlan` (its size, and the house and lawn in the middle), `LotArea`, and `Lot` with its `LotItem`s, everything built and planted, with `animal` (and `isFoals`) for a bed of the Conservationist's animals. The server uses this module too, so the two can't drift apart. |
| `ALifeClient` | What a front end uses. `ServerConnection` follows every session on a server: `connect()`, `setServerURL(_:)`, `startEverySessionOver()`, `phase`, `sessions`, one `AgentSession` per character, `lot`, what's built and planted, and `usage`, how much of the 5-hour window is used, and `models`, the `ModelOption`s for the model picker. An `AgentSession` is the observable handle on one character: `send(_:)`, `interrupt()`, `respond(to:allow:)`, `setAutoApprove(_:)`, `setModel(_:)`, `startOver()`, and the state to draw (`status`, `resumesAt` while it waits for the usage limit to reset, `activity`, `transcript`, `conversation`, `pendingApprovals`, `isWaitingForYou`, `autoApprove`, `subagents`, `context`, `model`, `fileModel`, `lastError`), and `modelName(among:)`, the model to show with its version, the one its session runs on or, before it starts, the one it will. A `SessionContext` (`context`) says what the context ring `measure`s against (Claude Code's compaction point, or the window), its `share`, `percent` and `band`, `tokensText` ("84k of 167k") and `spokenTokens`, and the `modelName` ("Opus 4.7"), or `modelName(among:)`, which also reads a simple name such as `opus` as the version it stands for. `ALifeServerClient` makes the HTTP calls and reads the event streams. `Narrator` says the characters' new lines (an `AgentSession`'s `newMessages`) out loud through a `SpeechEngine`, while they are within `Earshot.distance` of you, keeps the lines you missed and turns them into a `Call` once the character's job is done (`calls`, `take(callFrom:)`, `hangUp(on:)`, and `hasSomethingToSay(_:)` to keep it at its desk till then), writing each call in the log. A job a character finished while you were away (an `AgentSession`'s `finishedWhileAway`, passed in a `Speaker`) is a missed call instead, which doesn't ring (`missedCalls`, `hasMissedCall(_:)`, `clearMissedCall(from:)`; `incomingCalls` and `isCalling(_:)` are the ones ringing), and a `HeardRepliesStoring` keeps the last reply you heard from each character between launches, so the same job isn't a missed call twice. `EventLog` (`ServerConnection`'s `log`) keeps what the characters did, oldest first: replies, helpers sent off and back, jobs finished or stopped, usage limits waited out and being told to carry on, requests for a yes or no, errors, calls and missed calls, each with when it happened and whether it was while you were away (`isWhileAway`), and remembers when you last looked (`unseen`, `unseenWhileAway`, which the badge counts, `markSeen()`). An `AgentSession` is `isCatchingUp` while it is being caught up on, or finishing a job it had before you came; what it does then goes to the log but not to its `newMessages`. `SpokenText` turns a message's Markdown into what is said. `OfficePlan` lays out the office: how many storeys it has for so many characters, which room is whose, by slot, where the lift is, and where a helper's sheep stands in its character's room. `Commute` decides when a character goes up to its room and when it comes back out: only once it has looked done for a second, so it stays at its desk through the moment between you answering its request and it running again. `DayClock` turns the date into the time of day on the lot, a five-hour day the same for everyone: anchored to a usage window (`init(window:)`), midnight as it starts and as it resets, holding at midnight after (`until`; the clouds' `runningPhase` carries on), or, with no window ever read, days that follow the date alone (`lot(for:)`); its `Sky` says where the sun and the moon are (`elevation`, `azimuth`) and how light it is (`daylight`, `glow` at dawn and dusk, `moonlight`, `starlight`). `SkyClock` eases the sky over from one clock to the next in `SkyClock.easing` (10 seconds), the short way round. A `UsageWindow` says whether it `isCurrent(at:)` and `isStale(at:)` (over `staleAfter`, half an hour), its `countdown(at:)` ("2h 14m") and `spokenCountdown(at:)`, its `percent` and its `band` for the ring's colour. `Clouds` says where the few clouds in the sky are (`cover`, and `light`, greyer at their base), how far they have drifted round the sky (`drift`, a whole number of turns a day, so they carry on from one day to the next) and how much they show (`opacity`, faint at night). `HorizonPlan` lays out the lot's box (the compass, the walls on the lot's edges and how tall they are, its twelve `edges` and how softly they are shaded, what is painted on the walls, and where the sky shows a direction), and `CameraReach` how the camera moves inside it: how far it tilts, looks up and zooms, and where the walls and the sky stop it; tests check that from every camera it allows, it is inside the box and everything it sees is the lot, a wall or the sky, that it keeps the point it turns round in view, and that it moves without jumps. |
